## Shape observations

The car is a broad, nearly parallel-sided GT body with a modest waist. It is not an hourglass. The front fenders flow into a rounded wedge nose; the rear fenders and rear wing are broad and relatively square. Mirror stalks protrude behind the front axle. A single exterior path should follow those outer features, without drawing the roof, cockpit, bonnet vents or internal panel lines.

In the BBR image, the nose is around x=80, the rear-wing back edge x=1515, the main body spans approximately y=250…890, and mirror tips extend to approximately y=200…930. These are visually estimated image-space coordinates. The full body-plus-wing length/body-width ratio is approximately 2.24. Rotate the silhouette 90° clockwise to put the nose at the top.

An actual projected rear wing has a small air gap from the main body. To satisfy a single continuous outline, use a simplified external envelope across that small gap. Do not add a separate rear-wing line over the body.

For visual axle placement, the front wheel centre lies approximately 23% of the length from the nose and the rear around 81%, based on the overhead shape and a 2.66 m wheelbase. This is a drawing estimate, not an extracted engineering coordinate.

## Dimensional cross-check

The [official iRacing Ferrari 296 GT3 manual, page 4](https://s100.iracing.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Ferrari-296-GT3-V4.pdf) lists length 4565 mm, width 2050 mm and wheelbase 2660 mm. The [SRO-published Ferrari press release](https://www.gt-world-challenge-europe.com/news/2378/ferrari-296-gt3-a-v6-for-a-new-sporting-history) confirms width 2050 mm without mirrors, wheelbase 2660 mm and front/rear tracks 1726/1710 mm. These establish useful drawing proportions. They do not make the sandbox's generic force model a Ferrari vehicle model.
